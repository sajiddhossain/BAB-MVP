The four skills you'll build

👀 Recognise

Feel and name what's happening inside — energy, focus, mood, hunger, tension, pain — and where it lives in your body.

🧩 Understand

Connect the signal to what caused it, and to where you are in your cycle. Predict, then check.

🗣️ Communicate

Put your signals into words for coaches, physios and parents — especially pain worth protecting.

🧭 Manage

Use your read to honour your effort, look after yourself in the moment, and protect a long, healthy life in sport.

One skill a month: Month 1 grows Recognise, Month 2 Understand, Month 3 Communicate, Month 4 Manage. Each builds on the last.

🧡 Made to be good for you

BAB never hands you a score or a verdict. Your felt sense always leads — a watch or app is only a second opinion you're learning to weigh, never the boss. The question here is always "how well did I read myself today?" — never "how fit or recovered am I?"

And BAB works fully on its own — you don't need a smartwatch to get everything from it. If you do wear one, you can connect it whenever you like, right from the start, as an extra second opinion to weigh against how you feel.

The science, simply

There's a real name for the skill of sensing what's happening inside you: interoception. It's your body's inner sense, and — this is the good part — it's trainable, like a serve or a jump. Scientists think your teenage years may be a golden window for building it, because the brain pathways for reading your body are still developing right now (Welsh et al., 2026).

The way you train it is a loop: predict how you'll feel, tune in and check, compare the two, and adjust. Being "wrong" isn't a fail — the gap between your guess and your body is exactly where learning happens. Over these four months, two things grow: your read gets accurate, and your quiet confidence that you can handle whatever your body throws at you — one of the strongest protectors against burnout and injury there is.

🌱 Rest is part of training — not the opposite

Here's a truth this whole journey keeps coming back to: you get stronger while you recover, not during the hard bit. So BAB helps you step away from the old "no pain, no gain" idea. Pushing through everything isn't toughness — it's how injuries and burnout start. Honouring a Gentle day, taking a real rest day, or easing off mid-session is a skill and a strength. The athletes who keep playing for years are the ones who learn to recover on purpose.

🌙 Your rhythm, underneath it all

After your first period, your body moves through four phases, and that rhythm touches everything — energy, hunger, mood, sleep and focus. Most tools ignore it completely. This journey puts it underneath everything, so the patterns you spot actually make sense. You set your recent period dates when you first open BAB, so it can map your phases from day one — and you keep it updated as you go, since at your age your cycle is often still settling and irregular. It's about noticing your own tendencies over time — never a fixed schedule.

This journey helps you understand yourself — it doesn't replace your coach, a physiotherapist, or a doctor, who can see and check things a screen can't. If anything ever feels wrong with your body or your mind, tell a trusted adult. That's always the strong move.
