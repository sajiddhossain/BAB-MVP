🌙 Your Rhythm

Get to know your monthly rhythm

Your body moves in a monthly rhythm, and it quietly touches everything — energy, hunger, mood, sleep, even how sore you feel. It's still finding its pattern right now, so we don't predict it; we just get to know it. Tap what feels true today, or skip it — no pressure.

What the different parts of your rhythm can feel like — all normal 👀

Everyone's different, and while your rhythm is still settling these are things you might notice — not a timetable to expect:

🩸 Your period (the bleeding days): energy can dip and cramps can show up — or you might feel totally fine. You lose a little iron now, so iron-rich food (meat, beans, lentils, leafy greens) really helps.

🌤️ The days after your period: energy often climbs and you may feel strong and sharp — a lovely window to push, if your channels agree.

🍫 The week or so before your period: the luteal munchies have entered the chat. Feeling hungrier, craving more, or a bit moody or bloated isn't just a vibe — it's real: your metabolism ticks up slightly in this phase, so your body genuinely needs more fuel. Feed it. Being "nutritionally emotional" is simply your body asking for what it needs to train, study and have fun.

🌱 Not started your period yet? Also completely normal — bodies begin on their own timeline, anywhere across these years.

Your rhythm isn't something to forecast (you really can't yet) — it's something to notice, fuel for, and be kind to yourself across. Over the months, you might start spotting your own patterns.

Decode your ache

Two very different things get called "pain"

Before deciding anything, just learn to name what you're feeling. Reading the sensation is the skill — what to do about it comes afterwards.

✅ Working & everyday ache

  • The burn of muscles working hard, and heavy breathing
  • Muscles feeling tight or stiff
  • Soreness that's fairly even on both sides
  • An ache that eases as you warm up
  • Dull, come-and-go aches — a crampy tummy or lower back, or a heavy, bloated feeling

🛡️ Protective pain

  • Sharp, stabbing or sudden
  • Inside a joint or a bone
  • On one side only, or makes you limp
  • Gets worse as you go, or hurts at night

The tell: working and everyday aches ease as you warm up and settle within a day or two. Protective pain sticks, sharpens, or changes how you move. When you're not sure, call it protective.
